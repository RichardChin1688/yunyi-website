Founder

This brand began with two hands

A brand’s character usually comes from the way it started.

“I started with my hands. Purely by hand. One pair of hands led a team, the team made products, and the products became a closed loop.”

She said it without pausing. She was not describing a business. She was describing a craft.

People often assume Yunyi began as a company. A company did come — Shenzhen Yunming Yuchen Culture Communication Co., Ltd. was registered in Shenzhen in 2016 and has traded continuously for nine years. But in her own account the real beginning was health, not business: first the craft in her hands, then people willing to follow, then products people could actually use, and only then a closed loop.

So the brand is best read in reverse order: a person first, products after.

“Do not compete with hospitals for the same meal. One side prevents, the other nourishes. Each does its own job.”

This is a line she returns to. Prevention and everyday care belong here; diagnosis and treatment belong to hospitals. It is not only compliance — it is her own definition of the craft: stay inside your lane, and go deep inside it.

ICraft

The craft came first

Hands first, then a team, then products. She keeps that order deliberately — products were carried out by the craft, not rushed out by capital.

IIProvenance

A formula should name its era

“Every product has a Chinese medicine foundation and provenance — we can say which era the classical formula comes from.” For her, provenance is not packaging talk.

IIIThe long road

Not cheaper — longer

“On price I concede; I cannot compete there, and please do not ask me to.” She skips the lowest tier and chases no trend; she cares whether the brand lasts.

Growing wormwood is slow work

On ingredients, the most concrete thing she describes is wormwood: “We contracted land — two plots. The wormwood is harvested at the Dragon Boat Festival, then left to purify with air moving through it, to ferment internally. The following year it is worked again, left another year, then branded and sealed. So ours is generally three years or more.”

Contracted land, harvest at the right festival, ageing, reworking — the process is neither quick nor cheap. She says it calmly: slow things are the ones that last.

One person, one formula — that is what is real

“Everyone’s constitution is different; the same formula does not suit everyone.” That is where the brand’s editorial stance comes from — knowledge and method in general only; never one answer for everybody.

She also likes a homely line: “Everyday wellness starts in your own kitchen.” Eat a little less and move a little more; start with vegetables. The old saying goes, “If you want a child to be well, keep them a little hungry and a little cool.” To her, the entrance to wellness is not a shelf — it is daily life.

On how to eat, she mentions the “211 plate”: two parts vegetables, one part protein, one part whole grains. It is no secret recipe — just a habit she hopes more people will keep.

“I want this brand to take root in people’s hearts, not merely to be used on your body.”

Nine years is not long, and she is in no hurry. She puts it plainly: the brand does not need to be fast; it needs to last.

How she practises the craft

On ingredients and formulas she says little, but everything lands on method: wormwood needs its own land, harvest at the right festival, ageing; a formula should name its provenance and its era. She does not say “it works” — she says “how it is made”.

On customers she is equally direct: “One person, one formula — that is what is real.” She believes constitutions differ, so one approach does not fit everyone. She would rather speak slowly than overshoot.

On partnership she is blunter still: “What I lack is not formulas, but people who buy the products.” The brand’s job, in her view, is to make good things and keep supply steady; selling belongs to those who have a route to market. That is why Yunyi is open to agency and distribution partners.

What she does not want to do

She has said clearly what she will not do: she will not compete in the lowest price tier, and she will not fight hospitals for their work. Both sound like concessions; they are really definitions. If you do not compete on price, you must be more solid elsewhere; if you do not cross into medicine, you must be genuinely professional inside your own lane.

“Do not compete with hospitals for the same meal. One side prevents, the other nourishes. Each does its own job.” She has repeated it more than once. For someone in wellness, drawing that line clearly is itself a professional attitude.

How she sees wellness

When she talks about wellness she rarely talks about products; she talks about daily life. “Everyday wellness starts in your own kitchen.” Eat a little less, move a little more, start with vegetables. The old saying: keep a child a little hungry and a little cool. And the “211 plate”.

To her, wellness is not a set of things to buy but a set of small things to do every day. Products make those small things a little easier to keep up.
